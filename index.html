About

I am a Ph.D. candidate in Computer Science and Applied Mathematics at Iowa State University. My research focuses on machine learning methods for scientific and decision-making systems, with particular interests in uncertainty quantification, out-of-distribution detection, reinforcement learning, causal inference, and differentiable simulation.

I am especially interested in building AI systems that can work with mechanistic models, reason under uncertainty, and accelerate scientific exploration in complex domains such as agriculture.

Research

My work spans four connected directions:

Scientific Machine Learning

Machine-learning surrogates and differentiable models that accelerate simulation, enable large-scale search, and support scientific discovery.

Uncertainty & Robustness

Uncertainty quantification, conformal prediction, and out-of-distribution detection for reliable decisions under distribution shift.

Sequential Decision Making

Reinforcement learning and optimization for decision-making in dynamic scientific and agricultural systems.

Causal & Statistical Learning

Causal inference under missing data, functional data analysis, and data-driven methods for complex observational systems.
